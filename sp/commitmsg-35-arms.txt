feat(flight): three more paired arms — the captured hour at LOW, and 3.5-lite at HIGH on both byte shapes

The 2026-09-18 bench put gemini-3.5-flash-lite at HIGH level with gemini-3.1-flash-lite at LOW
on quality: +3 acceptable on 117 pairs, 15 up / 12 down, inside the +/-4 noise floor, while each
model beat the pre-bench default by a similar margin (+18 and +21). Two differences did show:
3.5 answers far shorter (words p50 74 vs 95; 44 of 117 over 85 words vs 72), and it looked
faster (p90 4.9 s vs 7.1 s, one answer over the 10 s stall budget vs five).

The length result is clean. The latency result is not: the 3.1 arms ran 2026-09-17 at 10:05 and
the 3.5 arms 2026-09-18 at 03:00, and free-tier latency swings with load (s50a measured 3.5-lite
at p50 19.9 s / p90 86 s during a busy hour). Answering that needs both models in one window.

So every flight now also runs:
  captured-low   the hour's captured prompts at LOW, the offline twin of the hour itself. Against
                 the in-app answers it isolates what the app pipeline adds over a raw API call,
                 and it is the like-for-like partner for captured-high.
  captured-high  the same captured prompts on 3.5-lite at HIGH — the head-to-head on app bytes
  high           the bare verbal prompt on 3.5-lite at HIGH — the head-to-head on bare bytes,
                 against the existing `low` arm
All five paired arms answer minutes after the hour, so nothing is compared across times of day.
About 155 lite calls, split across the two models' separate daily quotas.

PAIRED_ARMS entries may now name either Flash Lite; answersFileFor already tags by model and tag,
so each arm keeps its own file and pairs file. A test guards that no 3.5 arm is ever set to LOW:
probes (n=2 per cell) had 3.5-lite reporting no thought tokens at LOW on 3 of 4 calls while
honouring MEDIUM and HIGH every time, so a 3.5 arm at LOW would silently measure the provider
default and be reported as a thinking arm. Watched that guard fail before relying on it.

Suite 690, tsc at the 6-error baseline.
