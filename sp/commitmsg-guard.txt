fix(verbal): the 200-word guard ends on a finished sentence instead of slicing one

Flight s50i (2026-09-18) cut S2Q07 at exactly 200 words, mid-clause, on "measuring both
retrieval accuracy—using metrics". The same prompt answered offline ran to 218 words, and the
grader marked the in-app answer weak for the ending it never reached. Of the seven questions the
live hour lost against its own offline twin — same bytes, same model, same thinking level — this
was the ONLY one with an identifiable mechanical cause; the rest are sampling.

The guard had limit, floor and ceiling all at 200, so the stream never left stream mode and was
sliced wherever word 200 fell. Moving the floor alone is not enough: the switch to sentence
buffering can only happen AT a sentence boundary, so a long sentence that STARTS below the floor
is still streamed and still sliced at the ceiling. The gap between floor and limit is the budget
the next whole sentence must fit in, so the floor goes to 120, leaving 80 words — comfortably
more than any sentence these answers produce, and above the p50 of 105 words, so the answers the
prompt actually asks for stream exactly as before.

Buffering has to read a sentence before it can know whether it fits, which would have drained
the provider stream that the stream-mode ceiling used to abandon early. So buffer mode now stops
as soon as the unterminated tail cannot fit under the ceiling: the sentence is already too long
to keep, and reading the rest of it only pays for text nobody will hear. The early IteratorClose
is preserved (WhatToAnswerLLM.budget: consumed < total).

Two cases still stop mid-sentence, both correctly — a single sentence longer than the
floor-to-limit budget, and an answer with no terminator anywhere. Neither has a boundary to keep.
The gate row still reports every cut as a finding.

Verified against the original symptom, not just synthetic text: replaying s50i's own 218-word
S2Q07 through the shipped filter gives 200 words ending "…using metrics" before, and 173 words
ending "…via a retrieval-augmented generation framework." after. Swept over all 79 real answers
that flight (39 offline, 40 live): answers cut 1 before and 1 after, cut mid-sentence 1 before
and 0 after, and exactly one answer's text changed — the defective one.

Tests: guard constant, a runaway stopping on its last finished sentence at every chunk size, a
crossing sentence dropped whole rather than truncated, the no-terminator case still stopping at
the ceiling, and the caller-level runaway. All watched failing first. Suite 692, tsc at the
6-error baseline.
