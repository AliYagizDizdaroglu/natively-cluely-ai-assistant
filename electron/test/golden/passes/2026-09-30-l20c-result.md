## How it was graded

- One blind batch of 299 answers: 38 items × 8 samples, minus 5 holes that were not sent to graders
  (br1 S1Q08; Live rep 1 S1Q02, S1Q02F and S1Q09F; the empty captured-prompt rep 2 answer to S2Q06).
- Four packets, so that all of a question's answers go to the same two graders. Eight graders, two per packet, each a
  fresh `claude-opus-5-5` agent. The model was read from every grader's transcript: all eight ran on
  `claude-opus-5-5` and nothing else (`grader-models.mjs`, calibrated on two Sonnet transcripts, which it rejects).
- The frozen s50k rubric, verbatim.
- The two graders agree on "acceptable" for 290 of 299 answers. The nine disagreements: S1Q05F (Live 3);
  S1Q10 (Live 3), S2Q01 (Live 1, Live 2, app in-app), S1Q06F (app in-app); S1Q04 (app rep 3); S2Q10F (Live 1),
  S2Q05F (br1).
- Independence: no grader opened another grader's verdicts or the key. One grader (packet C, second grader) listed
  the folder once and so saw the NAMES of two other verdict files; it opened neither
  (`grader-independence.mjs` over all eight transcripts).
- The scorer (`score.mjs`) was calibrated before the real verdicts existed, on eight made-up verdict sets with known
  outcomes (`cal-score.mjs`, 8 cases).

## What this does not show

- One evening's load on Google's side, in two windows (17:21 and 21:00), and synthetic voices.
- Bare 3.8 Live: no thinking level set, no tuning of its instruction for this task. A different instruction could do
  better; that would be a new test with its own rule.
- The comparator's four samples come from the 2026-09-22 build. br1 shows today's build in the same range.
- 38 items detect only large differences. This one is large: 13.5 points between the means, and Live's best rep is
  below three of the app's four samples.
- The racer itself was never built, so nothing here measures how a race would behave.
